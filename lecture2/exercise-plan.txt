LECTURE 2 EXERCISE PLAN
=======================

Title
-----
Three Problems at Swineboils: Margins, Networks, and the Allocation Helm

Format
------
- After approval, deliver one student-facing notebook at
  lecture2/exercise.ipynb plus local copies of the two Allocation Helm CSV files.
- The notebook will run without network access or API keys.
- Dependencies: NumPy, pandas, Matplotlib, and scikit-learn. The neural network
  will be implemented with NumPy rather than hidden behind PyTorch.
- Intended classroom time: about 3.5-4 hours. Each task ends at a useful stopping
  point, so partial completion is acceptable.
- Every executable block will be labelled either RUN ONLY or TODO. The notebook
  will supply short tests, plots, and experiment bookkeeping, but no hints,
  solution sketches, recommended configurations, or leading questions.

Setting
-------
The notebook contains three unrelated incidents at the same magical school. The
shared Swineboils setting gives the exercise a coherent tone, but the problems do
not form one mission and solving one does not advance or unlock another:

1. The alchemy department must classify unstable potion batches.
2. The library must repair an unreliable handwritten-rune reader under a limited
   upgrade budget.
3. The admissions office must solve the original Allocation Helm decision
   problem from LessWrong.

Task 1 - Unstable potions (about 55 minutes)
--------------------------------------------
Concepts: SVM margins and kernels, scaling, model capacity, train/validation
behaviour, and choosing a simple model when the evidence supports it.

- Supply a small, reproducible, multi-feature binary dataset. Feature scales are
  deliberately very different and some labels are noisy. The generator has a
  mostly linear hidden signal, but the student notebook will not state or imply
  that fact.
- Students visualize selected feature pairs and standardize using statistics
  computed from the training split only.
- TODOs: standardize the data, compare several allowed SVM and neural-network
  configurations, calculate train/validation results, and justify a final model.
  The notebook defines the available tools and evaluation rules without
  suggesting which kernel, capacity, or hyperparameters should win.
- A supplied plotting shell lets students visualize a selected two-feature slice;
  students decide which trained boundaries or diagnostics are worth adding.
- Intended hidden design result: the linear SVM generalizes at least as well as
  flexible candidates while a large MLP can memorize much of the tiny training
  set. Exact thresholds will be calibrated during implementation and kept out of
  the student-facing instructions.
- Reflection: Which model should be deployed, and what evidence supports that
  choice beyond training accuracy?

Task 2 - The library rune reader (about 100 minutes)
----------------------------------------------------
Concepts: MLP forward pass, backpropagation, vanishing/exploding gradients,
initialization, mini-batch SGD, learning rates, convergence, and residual paths.

- Use scikit-learn's bundled 8x8 handwritten-digits dataset, presented as damaged
  magical runes. Loading, stratified train/validation/test splitting, flattening,
  scaling, plotting, and batching helpers are supplied.
- Supply a small NumPy neural-network framework. Students implement the key
  learning pieces: dense forward pass, activation derivatives, softmax
  cross-entropy gradient, dense-layer backward pass, and the SGD update.
  Assertions check shapes and numerical gradients on tiny examples.
- The starter network is intentionally too deep and narrow and should fail to
  reach a useful validation accuracy. Its full configuration is visible, but the
  notebook will not identify the cause of failure or recommend a repair.
- Students produce and interpret loss, accuracy, and per-layer gradient-norm
  diagnostics without being told what pattern they should find.
- Students may redesign the network by removing layers, widening layers, adding
  residual connections, changing activation, changing weight initialization,
  adjusting learning rate or batch size, or training longer.
- A supplied cost function charges for parameter count, premium activations,
  residual paths, initialization, and training epochs. The challenge is to reach
  at least 90% validation accuracy at minimum cost, then freeze the design and
  reveal test accuracy once. The leaderboard reports test accuracy, resource
  cost, and a combined efficiency score.
- Hidden acceptance target: the starter stays clearly below 90% while several
  low-cost solutions exceed it in a short CPU run. Those successful recipes will
  not appear in the student notebook.
- Reflection: Diagnose the starter model from the evidence, then identify which
  change improved generalization per unit cost rather than only training results.

Task 3 - The Allocation Helm (about 75 minutes)
-----------------------------------------------
Concepts: turning a decision into supervised learning, nonlinear regression,
distribution shift, observational bias, validation design, and optimizing the
actual downstream objective rather than model accuracy alone.

- Adapt the original LessWrong scenario and use local copies of its historical
  records and twenty incoming students. Preserve attribution and link to the
  original puzzle, but do not reveal its published solution before submission.
- Students inspect how class size, allocation patterns, and Ofspev ratings change
  over time. The notebook presents the observations but does not interpret which
  periods or rows are most useful.
- TODOs: decide how to represent House, choose a defensible validation scheme,
  build and compare rating models from the techniques available in the notebook,
  and predict every incoming student under all four possible Houses. The notebook
  will not point students toward a particular time period, model family, or
  treatment of the historical records.
- Students freeze one House choice per student, display an allocation table, and
  report the model's predicted average Ofspev rating.
- A clearly separated SPOILER / FINAL EVALUATION section reveals the true scoring
  rule only after choices are frozen and reports the class score. Random play
  scores about 17.8, the published optimum is about 24.9, and student submissions
  can compete on achieved average rating.
- Final reflection: Why can the model with the lowest validation error still make
  worse allocations, and how did the Helm's changing behaviour affect the data?

What students implement
-----------------------
- Standardization and open-ended model comparison for the SVM task.
- The concept-bearing NumPy MLP operations and mini-batch SGD updates.
- Architecture and training experiments under the explicit cost budget.
- House encoding, validation design, candidate outcome prediction, and the final
  allocation policy.

What the notebook supplies
--------------------------
- Reproducible data generation/loading and fixed dataset partitions.
- Plotting shells, tables, minibatch plumbing, experiment logs, and assertions.
- SVM estimator plumbing and an extensible but compact NumPy MLP scaffold.
- A final scorecard and guarded test/reveal sections to discourage test leakage.
- No hints or staged disclosures that suggest the intended model choices.

Implementation acceptance checks
--------------------------------
- Run a completed internal copy from top to bottom with fixed seeds.
- Verify numerical-gradient checks, finite losses, and correct tensor shapes.
- Confirm Task 1 rewards the simple linear model for the intended hidden reason.
- Confirm the Task 2 starter misses 90%, at least two affordable repairs pass 90%,
  and the full notebook has reasonable CPU runtime.
- Confirm no validation/test statistics leak into preprocessing or training.
- Confirm Allocation Helm scores reproduce the published random and optimal
  reference values to rounding.
